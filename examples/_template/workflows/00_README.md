# Workflows

> Define how your organization consistently delivers value.

Workflows describe the repeatable processes your organization uses to execute its Services.

A Workflow translates organizational knowledge into action by combining people, AI, playbooks, and tools into a consistent way of working.

Workflows should be practical, repeatable, and continuously improved through real-world execution.

---

# Purpose

The purpose of a Workflow is to make successful execution repeatable.

Rather than relying on individual experience, organizations should document how work is performed so that knowledge can be shared, improved, and reused.

Every Workflow should support one or more Services.

---

# Workflow Structure

Each Workflow should describe:

- Objective
- Scope
- Inputs
- Outputs
- Roles and Responsibilities
- Process Steps
- Deliverables
- Success Indicators
- Continuous Improvement

Keep Workflows focused on how work is performed—not why the organization exists.

---

# Relationship to Services

Services describe **what** your organization delivers.

Workflows describe **how** those Services are delivered.

```text
Purpose
      │
      ▼
Services
      │
      ▼
Workflows
      │
      ▼
Execution
```

A single Service may use multiple Workflows.

A single Workflow may support multiple Services.

---

# Relationship to Playbooks and Workers

Workflows orchestrate organizational capability.

```text
Knowledge
      │
      ▼
Playbooks
      │
      ▼
Workers
      │
      ▼
Workflow
      │
      ▼
Execution
```

- Knowledge provides context.
- Playbooks provide guidance.
- Workers perform specific tasks.
- Workflows coordinate execution.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Every completed Workflow should generate learning.

Capture improvements through:

- Case Studies
- Organizational Memory
- ADRs
- Updated Playbooks
- Improved Workflows

Execution should strengthen the organization over time.

---

# Tips

- Focus on repeatability.
- Keep steps clear and actionable.
- Assign responsibilities explicitly.
- Improve Workflows based on evidence.
- Remove unnecessary complexity.

A Workflow is successful when different people can achieve consistent outcomes by following it.
