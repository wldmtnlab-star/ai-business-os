# Constitution

> Define the principles that guide every organizational decision.

A Constitution describes the values and principles that should remain stable, even as strategies, technologies, products, and markets evolve.

If Purpose explains **why** your organization exists, the Constitution explains **how** it should behave.

---

# Purpose Alignment

Every principle should support your organization's Purpose.

Before writing your Constitution, make sure your Purpose is clearly defined.

A strong Constitution provides consistency during growth and change.

---

# Core Principles

List the principles that guide your organization.

Examples:

## Purpose Before Process

Purpose should guide every important decision.

Processes may change.

Purpose should remain consistent.

---

## Knowledge Is Shared

Knowledge belongs to the organization.

Avoid creating knowledge silos.

Document important decisions and make learning reusable.

---

## Humans Lead

AI supports people.

Technology should amplify human capability—not replace human responsibility.

---

## Learn Continuously

Every project should improve the organization.

Capture lessons learned.

Refine knowledge, workflows, and playbooks over time.

---

## Evidence Before Assumptions

Use observations, feedback, and measurable outcomes to guide decisions.

Avoid relying solely on opinions.

---

# Decision Making

Describe how important organizational decisions should be made.

Questions to consider:

- Who is responsible for major decisions?
- How should disagreements be resolved?
- What principles should never be compromised?

---

# Behaviors

Describe the behaviors your Constitution encourages.

Examples:

- Collaborate openly.
- Document important decisions.
- Share knowledge.
- Respect different perspectives.
- Improve systems instead of blaming people.

---

---

# Tips

- Keep the number of principles small.
- Make each principle actionable.
- Avoid vague or aspirational language.
- Write principles your team can remember and apply.
- Review them periodically, but change them only when truly necessary.

Your Constitution should guide decisions—not decorate documents.
