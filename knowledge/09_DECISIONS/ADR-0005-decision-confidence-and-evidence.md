# ADR-0005

# Decision Confidence Depends on Evidence Strength

## Context

Organizations make decisions under varying levels of uncertainty.

As AI becomes increasingly involved in research, analysis, and recommendation, it is easy to confuse confidence with correctness.

High confidence without sufficient evidence increases organizational risk.

A consistent decision-making principle is required to ensure that confidence reflects the quality of available evidence.

---

## Decision

Decision confidence shall never exceed the strength of the available evidence.

Evidence must be evaluated before confidence is assigned.

When evidence is weak, confidence must remain appropriately low, regardless of intuition, authority, or AI-generated certainty.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- More transparent decision making.
- Reduced organizational risk.
- Better handling of uncertainty.
- Increased trust in AI-assisted decisions.
- Encourages evidence-based culture.

### Trade-offs

- Decisions may require additional research.
- Some decisions may take longer.
- Uncertainty must be communicated explicitly.

---

## Principle

Evidence precedes confidence.

Confidence reflects evidence.

Uncertainty is acknowledged, not ignored.
