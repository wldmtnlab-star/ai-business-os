# ADR-0009

# Workers Are Accountable Execution Interfaces

## Context

Traditional organizations define work through job titles and organizational hierarchies.

As AI becomes an active participant in execution, these human-centric definitions become insufficient.

AI Business OS requires a model that can represent Humans, AI, and Hybrid teams using a consistent operational structure.

A Worker should therefore represent an execution capability rather than a person or a position.

---

## Decision

A Worker is defined as an accountable execution interface responsible for transforming organizational capabilities into meaningful outcomes.

Every Worker combines:

- Capability
- Responsibility
- Decision Authority
- Artifact Ownership

Workers execute shared Playbooks to produce organizational artifacts.

A Worker may be performed by:

- Human
- AI
- Hybrid Human-AI collaboration

The definition of a Worker remains constant regardless of its implementation.

---

## Consequences

### Positive

- Human and AI execution follow the same organizational model.
- Responsibilities become explicit and measurable.
- Organizational capabilities become reusable.
- Workers remain stable even as technology evolves.
- Execution is centered on outcomes rather than roles.

### Trade-offs

- Traditional job titles become secondary to execution responsibilities.
- Organizations must clearly define Worker boundaries and ownership.
- Some existing organizational structures may require adaptation.

---

## Principle

Capabilities define work.

Workers execute capabilities.

Artifacts create value.

Execution creates outcomes.

---

Responsibility defines a Worker, not a title.
