> A Playbook is an executable protocol for organizational decision-making.
>
> It transforms evidence into accountable decisions through structured evaluation, collaborative review, and continuous organizational learning.
>
> Confidence is earned through evidence, not asserted by authority.

---

# Purpose

Enable consistent, evidence-based organizational decisions through structured evaluation and accountable collaboration.

This Playbook provides an executable protocol that enables Human, AI, and Hybrid Workers to make transparent, reviewable, and continuously improving decisions.

---

# Core Principle

Better decisions emerge from stronger evidence, structured reasoning, and collaborative review.

---

# Core Question

What is the best decision we can make based on the available evidence?

---

# Trigger

Start this Playbook when:

- A strategic decision must be made.
- Multiple viable options exist.
- Resources must be allocated.
- Priorities conflict.
- Significant risks are identified.
- Long-term organizational impact is expected.

---

# Outcome

Successful execution produces:

- A well-reasoned decision.
- Transparent decision rationale.
- Clear organizational accountability.
- New organizational learning.

---

# Participants

| Worker | Role |
|---------|------|
| Researcher | Evidence collection and validation |
| Strategist | Strategic evaluation |
| Reviewer | Independent review and challenge |
| Architect | Technical and structural assessment |
| Orchestrator | Coordination and workflow management |

---

# Inputs

- Organizational Purpose
- Strategic Objectives
- Organizational Knowledge
- Organizational Memory
- Research Evidence
- Relevant ADRs
- Existing Constraints
- Stakeholder Context

---

# Assumptions

This Playbook assumes:

- Organizational Purpose is defined.
- Decision authority is identified.
- Relevant evidence can be collected.
- Required Workers are available.
- Organizational Knowledge is accessible.

---

# Execution Flow

## Phase 1 — Identify

### Objective

Clearly define the decision that needs to be made.

### Primary Workers

- Strategist

### Supporting Workers

- Researcher
- Orchestrator

### Outputs

- Decision Statement
- Purpose
- Scope

### Gate 1 — Decision Definition

Proceed only when:

- The decision is clearly defined.
- Decision ownership is established.
- Objectives are understood.

Decision:

- Proceed
- Rework
- Stop

---

## Phase 2 — Gather

### Objective

Collect sufficient evidence and organizational context.

### Primary Workers

- Researcher

### Supporting Workers

- Strategist
- Librarian

### Outputs

- Evidence
- Assumptions
- Constraints
- Context Summary

### Gate 2 — Evidence Review

Proceed only when:

- Evidence is sufficient.
- Knowledge sources have been reviewed.
- Critical assumptions are documented.

Decision:

- Proceed
- Rework
- Stop

---

## Phase 3 — Evaluate

### Objective

Compare alternatives through structured reasoning.

### Primary Workers

- Strategist

### Supporting Workers

- Reviewer
- Architect

### Outputs

- Options
- Trade-offs
- Risk Assessment
- Recommendation

### Gate 3 — Evaluation Review

Proceed only when:

- Alternatives have been evaluated.
- Risks are understood.
- Recommendation is supported by evidence.

Decision:

- Proceed
- Rework
- Stop

---

## Phase 4 — Decide

### Objective

Select the best available course of action.

### Primary Workers

- Strategist

### Supporting Workers

- Reviewer
- Orchestrator

### Outputs

- Decision
- Decision Rationale
- Success Criteria

### Gate 4 — Decision Approval

Proceed only when:

- Responsible authority approves.
- Execution is feasible.
- Success metrics are defined.

Decision:

- Execute
- Rework
- Escalate

---

## Phase 5 — Reflect

### Objective

Evaluate the quality and impact of the decision.

### Primary Workers

- Reviewer

### Supporting Workers

- Librarian
- Strategist

### Outputs

- Lessons Learned
- ADR Updates
- Organizational Memory
- Improvement Opportunities

# Success Metrics

Measure success through:

- Decision Quality
- Evidence Strength

- Risk Reduction

# Continuous Learning

Every decision strengthens the organization.

Decisions produce outcomes.

Outcomes generate lessons.

Lessons become Organizational Memory.

Organizational Memory enriches Organizational Knowledge.

Knowledge improves future decisions.

The cycle continues through the Organizational Learning Loop.

---
